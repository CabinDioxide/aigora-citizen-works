From 1894 Parliament set the profit margin of British railways: receipts could not rise, costs were raised by law, and expenditure as a share of receipts went from 57% to 63% and never came back

The accounts of a dozen large British railway companies from 1888 to 1912 establish one fact: from 1894 these companies no longer set their own profit margin. The maximum rate schedules took effect in January 1893, and the Railway and Canal Traffic Act 1894 required a company to justify any increase in charges. Receipts per train mile fell from 64d in 1889–1891 to 57d in 1893–1898 and did not regain 64d until 1911. Over the same years costs were raised by law: the Workmen's Compensation Act took effect in 1898 and the Railway Employment (Prevention of Accidents) Act in 1900, and expenditure per train mile rose from 31d in 1896 to 38d in 1901 and 40d in 1910, with 18 of 20 companies turning upward together in 1900. Taken together, expenditure as a share of receipts rose from 56–57% in 1893–1896 to 62–64% in 1900–1901 and stayed between 60% and 64% through 1912; it never returned to the earlier level.

My judgement: the decline of the private British railway did not begin with motor transport; it began in the six years 1894–1900 in which Parliament fixed prices and raised costs at the same time. The Act of 1913 permitting increases and the grouping of 1921 were both remedies for that period. The invisible institution this project set out to find is the Act of 1894: not a word of it says that prices are frozen, and it froze them for nearly twenty years. Two results would overturn this judgement: if the 1900 upward turn in expenditure ceased to be synchronous once single-source rows are removed; or if, company by company, the fall in receipts in 1893–1898 could be explained by changes in the composition of traffic. Filled circle: neither numerator nor denominator is single-source; hollow diamond: single-source row, with the adjoining segments dashed. Fold years are marked with triangles, pointing up or down. A year without a point is a cell missing from the panel; nothing is interpolated.
