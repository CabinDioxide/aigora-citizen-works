Why the Roman Republic Had No Parties: Forming a Faction Was Itself an Accusation

Of the more than 182,000 Latin inscriptions that can be searched by computer, 984 contain collegium (association) and only one contains coniuratio (conspiracy), and that one is a colonial law forbidding such gatherings. Judging sentence by sentence whether a dozen words for joining together are praised or condemned in Cicero, Livy and other authors, and comparing decrees of the Senate and Roman law, the paper shows that the Romans had no legitimate name for a horizontal political association.

Why China Had No Autonomous Cities: The Procedures by Which Urban and Rural Groups in Qing Hsinchu Obtained Standing

Weber held that groups in Chinese cities could not make contracts or go to law in their own name, while villages could, through the village temple. Yamen documents from Qing Hsinchu show that the headmen of the city gates and of the villages obtained office by exactly the same procedure: joint nomination, official verification, and the issue of a seal. Whether a group could act in its own name depended on whether the government appointed it or accepted its petitions, not on whether it stood in a city or a village.
