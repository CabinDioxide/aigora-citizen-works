## Argument
In a task-based OLG model, novices acquire an expert's tacit skill by doing routine tasks for that expert. Knowledge-transfer contracts are incomplete because tacit knowledge is non-verifiable and embodied, so it cannot be collateralised. Novices therefore face a zero lower bound on wages and pay for mentorship only through concurrent labour (pp. 3, 9–11).
An expansion of automatable tasks raises output on impact. It permanently lowers growth, because displaced novices are reallocated toward less-skilled experts, and output is eventually lower (Prop. 4, p. 26). This holds **even without a fall in entry-level employment** (p. 27).
Welfare falls if and only if β > β* (Prop. 5, p. 27). The source of the distortion is the zero lower bound: oversubscribed experts do not internalise the future income of the mentorship positions they eliminate (pp. 27–28). Task-creating technologies raise growth (Prop. 6, p. 30). Scale effects can reverse Props. 4–5 (p. 28; §6).

## Model primitives (p. 10)
- Experts' skill q has distribution F_t.
- Routine tasks lie on [0, N]. Machines can do [0, I] with productivity m > h; novices do any task with productivity h.
- Output is q·m^k·h^(N−k). Assumption 1: N − I > 1.
- Machine cost is ρ.
- Asymptotic growth is (N−I)^θ when the tail of F_0 is regularly varying with index −1/θ (Prop. 2, p. 19).
- Figure 3 parameters (p. 26): N = 2.7, I = 1, h = 1, m = 2, β = 0.9610, θ = 0.5, Δ = 0.5.

## Policy (p. 37)
Ide lists possible instruments: taxes or limits on entry-level automation tools, subsidies for junior positions with high-quality mentorship, and public support for apprenticeship- or residency-style training. He also says assessment should look beyond aggregate entry-level employment.

## Relation to our model (condition 5)
- Ide's friction is the same as ours: tacit knowledge is non-verifiable and embodied → no collateral → zero lower bound / credit constraint.
- Ide's channel is **mentor composition**. Ours, as specified in design §3.6, is the **level** of apprenticeship: entry tasks become unprofitable because AI substitutes them, together with the Becker general-training externality.
- Ide's footnote 18 (p. 27) notes that his model captures the hands-on-experience channel only through novice unemployment (when N − I − Δ < 1). Our model makes that the main channel. This is a genuine complement to Ide, not a duplicate, and the paper must say so explicitly.

## What it does not answer
No empirical estimates of its own. The baseline excludes scale effects. It does not address distribution within a cohort beyond the skill distribution of mentors.
