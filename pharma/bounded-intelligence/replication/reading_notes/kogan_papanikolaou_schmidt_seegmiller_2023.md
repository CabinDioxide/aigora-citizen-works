# Reading note — Kogan, Papanikolaou, Schmidt, Seegmiller, "Technology and Labor Displacement"

## Bibliographic record (verified)
Leonid Kogan, Dimitris Papanikolaou, Lawrence D. W. Schmidt, Bryan Seegmiller. "Technology and Labor Displacement: Evidence from Linking Patents with Worker-Level Data." NBER Working Paper 31846, November 2023, **revised November 2025**. doi:10.3386/w31846 (Crossref; read 2026-10-14). Version read: the NBER PDF as of 2026-10-14, which is the November 2025 revision.

## Pages
Printed page = PDF page − 2. Read: abstract; introduction; the main results on labour-saving and labour-augmenting exposure (pp. 22–30).
**Not read line by line:** the measurement section (patent and task text-matching), the industry-spillover section, and the appendices.

## Argument
The authors build patent-text measures of labour-saving and labour-augmenting technology exposure and link them to US administrative worker data.
- Labour-saving exposure lowers exposed workers' earnings broadly.
- Labour-augmenting exposure raises the group wage bill through employment, and raises entrants' earnings, but **lowers incumbents' earnings**. The incumbent decline is concentrated among white-collar, older and higher-paid workers (abstract), consistent with vintage-specific human capital.

## Relevance
- Supports the point (Papanikolaou's comment in the volume, ch. 8) that new tasks may favour entrants over incumbents.
- **For condition 3:** task creation can benefit entrants, but with a cost to incumbents' vintage-specific human capital.
- **For condition 5:** tacit, vintage-specific capital depreciates.
- The paper is not about generative AI (the period studied predates it).
