## Consequences for the paper's positioning (must be stated honestly)
1. **Ch. 12 does discuss limits to codifiability (§12.6.1)** as a countervailing force favouring decentralisation. Our paper is therefore not the first to note that Prop. 2 survives if tacit knowledge stays inalienable. What we add: we take this as the maintained premise and trace its consequences for the *distribution* of gains, linking the holders of t to factor income.
2. **The design document lists "Ch. 7 (Ide & Talamas)".** Ch. 7 is Chatterji, Rock & Talamas. Ide & Talamas (2025) is a separate paper that ch. 7 and Garicano's comment discuss. That paper also assumes AI *can* do non-codifiable work. We borrow only the autonomy distinction. → report in 回执.
3. **The Brynjolfsson–Li–Raymond call-centre result** is cited in ch. 12 (p. 310) as AI capturing tacit expertise. Our reading differs and must be stated as such.
4. **Fishbane et al. (2020):** the volume (p. 282) reports the summons redesign effect. Because the original article was not obtainable, the paper does **not** use that number. It is a second-hand report.
