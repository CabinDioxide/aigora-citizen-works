# Referee report — Proposition 4 (Autonomy and packaging cost)

**Shared hidden assumption (applies to i–iii).** The result w_j = s_j − C(D) (or s_j − C(D) − r) gives the *entire* marginal surplus to the worker. "Free entry into opportunity creation" by itself only forces *aggregate* entrepreneur profit to zero; it does not by itself force the *type-by-type* zero-profit condition used here. That stronger conclusion needs an unstated assumption: entrepreneurs observe z_j and Bertrand-compete for each worker individually (any entrepreneur can match any worker, so competition is type-specific, not just aggregate). Without it, a pooling equilibrium with a single posted wage and random matching is also consistent with free entry and zero *average* profit, and wages would not satisfy the stated formula. I also flag that risk-neutrality and a zero outside option for non-working holders are used but never stated.

**4(i).** With r* = 0 (justified: non-autonomous opportunities use at most H ≤ μ compute units, so compute is never scarce — this part is clean) and Bertrand-per-type competition among entrepreneurs, zero profit gives w_j = s_j − C(D) when positive, 0 otherwise, i.e. w_j = max{z_A+(1−z_A)z_j − C(D), 0}. Employment holds iff z_j > z* = (C(D)−z_A)/(1−z_A). This is arithmetically correct and I can reproduce it line for line. Minor boundary note: at z_j = z* exactly, both employment and non-employment give w_j = 0, so "iff z_j > z*" vs "≥" is a labeling choice, not a substantive error. Also worth flagging: if C(D) < z_A, the formula gives z* < 0, meaning the threshold never binds (everyone is employed); this is not wrong, but the proposition doesn't note that this is precisely the boundary at which case (iii) takes over, which would be worth one sentence for continuity.

**4(ii).** Given (i)'s formula, ∂w_j/∂D = c₁/D² is a direct consequence of C′(D) = −c₁/D², correct. ∂z*/∂D = C′(D)/(1−z_A) < 0, so the threshold falls — correct. For employed j,k with s_j > s_k, the ratio R(D) = (s_j−C(D))/(s_k−C(D)) has dR/dC = (s_j−s_k)/(s_k−C)² > 0, and C is decreasing in D, so R is strictly decreasing in D — I verified this holds for *all* C < s_k (not just a special case), so the "compression" claim is general, not an artifact. ∂w_j/∂z_A = 1−z_j ≥ 0 follows immediately from differentiating s_j in z_A. All correct, conditional on (i)'s formula (hence inherits the Bertrand-per-type caveat above).

**4(iii).** Given C(D) < z_A, AI-only opportunities are profitable at r = 0, and since compute supply is fixed at μ with zero marginal cost to the owner, free entry into AI-only opportunities bids r up until z_A − C(D) − r = 0, i.e. r* = z_A − C(D). Substituting into the human-assisted zero-profit condition s_j − C(D) − r* − w_j = 0 gives w_j = s_j − z_A = (1−z_A)z_j, independent of D — the C(D) terms cancel exactly, which I verified algebraically; this is a genuine and correct result, not a near-cancellation. ∂w_j/∂z_A = −z_j ≤ 0 is immediate and correctly signed, and the sign-flip relative to (ii) (+1−z_j there vs. −z_j here) is the paper's real content and is sound: better autonomous AI raises the entrepreneur's outside option (AI-only), which is captured by compute owners via r*, not by human-assisted workers.

Two things the statement should but does not say:
1. **Full employment in (iii).** Since w_j = (1−z_A)z_j ≥ 0 for every z_j ≥ 0 with equality only at z_j = 0, the employment threshold collapses to (essentially) zero — every holder with positive knowledge is employed, in contrast to the partial-employment threshold z* in (i). This is a substantively important comparative-static result (autonomy expands employment at the extensive margin while compressing wage *gains* relative to AI improvement) and its absence from the proposition's text makes the stated claim strictly weaker than what the model actually delivers.
2. **Indeterminate quantity of AI-only opportunities.** The zero-profit condition pins the *price* r* but not the *quantity* of AI-only opportunities created (standard under constant returns/free entry), so long as it stays within compute capacity μ − H. Not an error, but worth a sentence, since a reader could otherwise think r* alone fully characterizes the equilibrium allocation.

**Fit with the standing "inclusive" definition.** The standing definition measures inclusiveness via EV(y)/y relative to a baseline income y. Proposition 4 never constructs an EV(y) or a baseline y for holder j (e.g., the no-AI wage), so "wages compress" (a statement about levels w_j, w_k) is not shown to imply, and is not obviously equivalent to, the formal EV/y criterion. If a later section equates "wage compression across z_j" with "inclusive in the standing sense," that step is missing here and should not be assumed free.

**Verdict on substance.** No sign errors, no false numerical claims; I could not construct a counterexample to any displayed formula given the stated primitives. The weaknesses are (a) an unstated but necessary market-structure assumption (type-specific Bertrand competition, zero-cost compute supply) that the formulas silently require, and (b) two under-stated implications (full employment in iii; indeterminate AI-only quantity) that make the proposition's content narrower than what the model actually implies.

VERDICTS:
4(i): TRUE-WITH-CAVEAT (requires type-specific/Bertrand competition among entrepreneurs, not just aggregate free entry, plus zero outside option)
4(ii): TRUE-WITH-CAVEAT (correct given 4(i)'s formula; inherits the same caveat)
4(iii): TRUE-WITH-CAVEAT (correct given zero-marginal-cost compute supply and free entry into AI-only opportunities; omits the implied full-employment result and leaves AI-only quantity indeterminate)
