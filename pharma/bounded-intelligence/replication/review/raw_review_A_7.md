# Referee Report — Proposition 7 (A.7 Liability)

**Setup check.** The certainty-equivalent formula given, RP(y) = (1/λ(y)) ln(1−π+π e^{λ(y)L}) − πL, is exactly the standard CARA risk premium for a Bernoulli loss (loss L w.p. π, 0 otherwise), derived from u(w−CE)=E[u(w−X)] with u(x)=−e^{−λx}/λ. This is correctly stated and RP>0 follows from Jensen's inequality for λ>0, 0<π<1, L>0.

**(i) — RP increasing in λ, decreasing in y.** The monotonicity of RP in λ (for a *fixed* lottery) is a global, not merely local, fact: it is Pratt's (1964) theorem — CARA utility with higher λ is an increasing concave transform of CARA utility with lower λ, so the risk premium for the same lottery is larger. Numerically, with π=0.1, L=100: λ=0.001 → RP≈0.462; λ=0.01 → RP≈5.85; λ=0.02 → RP≈14.70 — monotone increasing, confirming the claim. Given λ′(y)<0, the chain rule gives RP′(y)=RP_λ·λ′(y)<0, so RP(y) is strictly decreasing in y. This part is a correct, verifiable mathematical fact.

The second half of (i) — "adds a term with negative slope to the effective-price schedule, and by Lemma 1 it pushes towards regressivity" — is *not independently checkable here*. We are given only the statement of Proposition 7, not Lemma 1's text, its exact gradient condition, or how "effective price" enters EV(y). A decreasing-in-y additive cost term need not translate into EV(y)/y increasing in y unless the benefit side B(y) and the way price enters EV (lump-sum vs. proportional, additive vs. multiplicative) are restricted appropriately. The inference is plausible but the proposition is importing an unverified external result; as stated it is a citation, not a proof step the reviewer can check.

**(ii) — Preference for pooling.** Self-bear cost is πL+RP(y); pooled cost is πL(1+ℓ)=πL+ℓπL. Comparing certainty-equivalent costs directly, pooled cost ≤ self-bear cost ⟺ ℓπL ≤ RP(y). This is a correct and complete derivation — no hidden step. Using the same numbers (λ=0.01, π=0.1, L=100, ℓ=0.2): RP≈5.85 > ℓπL=2, so this household prefers pooling; with λ=0.001, RP≈0.462 < 2, so that (less risk-averse, i.e. richer) household prefers self-bearing. Since RP(y) is continuous and strictly decreasing (given λ(y) continuous and strictly decreasing, which the premises assert only as λ′<0 — differentiability already implies continuity, so this is fine), the super-level set {y : RP(y) ≥ ℓπL} is indeed a lower interval of the income support. The claim holds, including degenerate cases (empty set, or the whole support) which are trivially intervals.

Two nitpicks: (a) "prefers" is stated with a weak inequality (≥); at equality the household is indifferent, not strictly preferring — the proposition should say "weakly prefers" or use strict inequality consistently. (b) The result implicitly assumes π and L are the *same* across the two regimes and across households (no y-subscript on π, L) — true per the given primitives, but worth flagging as a substantive restriction (no adverse selection or moral hazard effect of liability-shifting on π or L).

**(iii) — Flatness under pooling.** Given the stated primitives, π, L, ℓ are exogenous constants without y-dependence, so πL(1+ℓ) is literally constant in y; its derivative w.r.t. y is zero by construction, and it "drops out" of any additively separable gradient condition. This is essentially a restatement of the premises rather than new content — it is true, but trivially so, and its connection to "the gradient conditions of Lemma 1" again cannot be verified without Lemma 1's text. If Lemma 1's gradient condition is not additively separable in the relevant term, "drops out" would need a separate argument.

**Hidden assumptions / ambiguities flagged:**
1. **Sign of λ(y) itself.** Only λ′(y)<0 is assumed; nothing restricts λ(y)>0 on the whole income support. If λ(y) passes through 0 (risk-neutral) or goes negative (risk-loving) for high y, RP(y) is not well-defined by the stated closed form in the usual sense (needs a limiting L'Hôpital value at λ=0) and can become negative for λ<0, contradicting "RP(y)>0" used elsewhere in the paper. This should be made an explicit maintained assumption (λ(y)>0 for all y in support).
2. **Reliance on an unstated Lemma 1.** Both (i)'s regressivity conclusion and (iii)'s "drops out of the gradient conditions" claim are not self-contained; they invoke Lemma 1's content, which is not supplied to the reviewer. As written, these are forward references, not proof steps, and cannot be certified as correct in isolation.
3. **"Prefers" vs. "weakly prefers"** in (ii) — minor wording looseness at the indifference point.
4. **Implicit assumption that π, L do not depend on the liability regime** — plausible but substantive (liability-shifting could itself change incentives to avoid error, i.e. moral hazard on π, or change the realized loss L via supplier remediation); the proposition silently rules this out.
5. **Interval claim in (ii)** is correct but only because RP is *globally* monotonic; if the paper later relaxes λ′<0 to hold only on average (not pointwise), the single-crossing/interval structure could fail — worth flagging since λ′<0 is used as a blanket premise elsewhere but is doing real work here.

VERDICTS:
1. (i) first clause (RP increasing in λ, decreasing in y): TRUE
1. (i) second clause ("pushes towards regressivity" via Lemma 1): TRUE-WITH-CAVEAT (depends on unstated Lemma 1 and on how price enters EV(y); not independently verifiable here)
2. (ii): TRUE-WITH-CAVEAT (correct as derived, modulo weak/strict preference wording at equality and the assumption π, L are regime-invariant)
3. (iii): TRUE-WITH-CAVEAT (flatness is immediate from the stated primitives; "drops out of Lemma 1's gradient conditions" cannot be checked without Lemma 1's text)
